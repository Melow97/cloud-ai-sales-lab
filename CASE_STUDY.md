# ATLAS — Sample Customer Solution Case Study

> **Independent educational simulation.** This is not a real customer engagement, production deployment, verified cost quotation or demonstrated sales result.

## Executive scenario
A fictional 200-person retail company wants to shorten customer-support resolution time without replacing its existing case-management platform. Repetitive order-status questions and inconsistent knowledge articles cause handoffs and long resolution times. Leadership wants measurable efficiency with clear information governance.

## 1. Stakeholder discovery

| Stakeholder | Discovery question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Customer support lead | Which request types have the highest volume and longest handling times? | Sets a measurable baseline |
| Finance leader | What does one handled interaction cost today? | Establishes the value hypothesis |
| IT / security | Where is approved knowledge stored, and what information cannot be exposed? | Constrains architecture and risk |
| Business sponsor | What pilot threshold would justify expanding adoption? | Defines a decision path |

## 2. Solution hypothesis
Evaluate an AI-assisted support workflow that retrieves approved support knowledge, drafts answers, provides case summaries and routes complex cases to specialists with human oversight.

**Microsoft solution conversation:** Microsoft Foundry and associated Azure services might be relevant, but exact service selection depends on architecture, data location, integration requirements, security controls, latency and cost. Engage a Cloud Solution Architect to confirm feasibility.

## 3. Hypothetical value model

Illustrative assumptions (not a customer quote):
- 45 affected specialists
- 6 hours potentially saved per specialist per month
- €38 fully loaded hourly labour cost
- €4,200 estimated monthly solution cost including ongoing operations

**Gross monthly labour value:** 45 × 6 × €38 = **€10,260**.
**Net illustrative monthly value:** €10,260 − €4,200 = **€6,060**.
**Illustrative annual ROI:** (€10,260 − €4,200) ÷ €4,200 × 100 = **144.3%**, assuming recurring months and ignoring any additional one-off costs.

**Critical limitation:** Hours saved are not automatic cash savings; the above is an opportunity-value hypothesis rather than realized revenue, reduced payroll or a guaranteed financial return. Real costs must include licensing, integration, implementation, support, security, review and adoption.

## 4. Pilot proposal (hypothetical)
1. Establish baseline ticket volume, handling time, resolution quality and escalation rate.
2. Select a low-risk set of approved knowledge articles and request categories.
3. Run a time-boxed proof of concept under IT security review, with human approval.
4. Compare handling time, accuracy, escalation and satisfaction against baseline.
5. Bring the sponsor, finance, operations and technical partners together for a go/no-go decision.

## 5. Risk and objection handling
- **“What if the model invents an answer?”** Limit approved sources, expose citations where appropriate, log results and require humans to review high-risk responses.
- **“Is there a guaranteed saving?”** No. Validate with a pilot and calculate ROI from observed usage and costs.
- **“Does it replace our existing system?”** Not necessarily. Integration options and operational fit must be assessed before an architecture is proposed.
- **“What about confidential data?”** Assess authorization, retention, identity, data handling, access controls and geographic requirements before implementation.

## 6. Commercial close / next commitment
Seek agreement to a discovery workshop, named technical owner, approved pilot scope, success criteria and a decision date. A successful pilot enables a justified proposal, not an automatic purchase.

## A 90-second interview explanation
“I created ATLAS to demonstrate my approach to Cloud and AI solution selling. Instead of leading with technology, I start with discovery: what the customer wants to improve, who owns the problem, and how we will measure the baseline. In this retail scenario, I map repetitive support workflows to an AI-assisted use case, then test the value using transparent assumptions. I would bring a technical specialist into the conversation to validate governance, integration and Azure pricing. Only after a controlled pilot demonstrates real outcomes would I propose expansion. My experience in technology sales and customer support helps me balance the commercial conversation with practical customer needs.”
